02 — Workflow

Structured thinking.
Intentional making.

Every project follows a deliberate pace — from diagnosing the real problem to shipping something that holds up. Here's how I move.

01

Understand

I start by digging into the actual business problem — not just the brief. Who are the users, what are the stakeholder goals, and where do they conflict? I don't open Figma until I know what I'm solving for.

02

Define

Once the problem is clear, I map out user flows, information architecture, and success criteria. This is where I set the guardrails — what done looks like, what trade-offs are acceptable, and what the roadmap should prioritize.

03

Design

Systematic, component-driven visual and interaction design in Figma. I think in systems — not screens. Every decision has a reason: spacing, hierarchy, color, motion. Nothing arbitrary.

04

Build

I bridge the gap between design and development. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — with AI as a teammate where it adds speed without sacrificing quality. I know enough about the build to catch what breaks in production before it ships.

05

Ship

Deploy, QA, document. Whether it's a clean Figma handoff to a dev team or a direct Vercel deployment, I make sure what launches matches what was designed — and that whoever touches it next can understand it.

06

Iterate

The work doesn't end at launch. I treat post-release feedback, usage data, and client input as part of the design process — refining, escalating, and building on what shipped until the product is genuinely better.

04 — Q&A

Thinking aloud.

A few questions worth answering — on design, process, and where I'm headed.

I bridge the gap between design and development. I don't just handoff wireframes and assets - I see a product from research and ideation all the way to development - and then back to iteration for improvement and scalability.

Over 8 years in UI/UX, I worked across industries like e-gaming, SaaS, e-commerce, and AI software so I learned to adapt my design approach depending on the product, market, and business objectives.

It usually starts with understanding the problem - talking to stakeholders, doing research, mapping the user journey. From there I move to drafting by wireframing, prototyping while establishing or using a design system.

On some projects, I'm also the one building and deploying the front-end and half of the backend via vibe-coding. I like learning the full circle whenever I can.

AI isn't just a shortcut for me - it's a tool I utilize by understanding deeply. I work with concepts such as LLMs, TAG, and MCPs to know where AI can add real value and also where it can mislead you.

In practice, I use it for research, context-gathering, and even vibe-coding with tools like Claude Code. But the direction still has to be mine.

I treat AI more of a technical colleague where it can fill my lack of technical expertise in development.
